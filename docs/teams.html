Nod for teams.

Your team uses AI tools on real code. Nod makes sure a person says yes before anything risky happens.

A-01 TEAMS
Psst. I’m Dibu. I guard the Nod server.

Signed by a person.

A bad instruction hidden in a web page or a file can talk an AI tool into almost anything. It can't press a button on your desk. With Nod for teams, a deploy or a force push only runs after a key signs for that exact action.

The key works today. The rest of this page is being built, and it's marked that way.

Signed approvalsEach press is signed by a secret that never leaves the key. Nothing on the laptop can fake one.Next
Rules in a fileDecide once what runs on its own, what needs a press and what needs two. The rules live with your code.Next
Two keys for the big onesA deploy to production waits for a second person on the team to press their key.Later
A record of who said yesEvery risky change, who approved it, and proof. Ready for reviews and audits.Later
# nod.rules  (example)
run on its own   npm test, pytest, git push origin feat/*
needs a press    git push origin main, npm publish
always blocked   rm -rf /, git push --force main
needs two keys   deploy prod, db migrate

What it’s built to stop

An AI tool running something risky without a person pressing a key. Once presses are signed, malware on a laptop faking a press. Someone swapping the action after you read it.

What it doesn't

Anything that never goes through Nod, like a password already saved on the laptop. That's why the keys that matter move to the Nod server.

A-03 SITE CHECKS
Holes in your site? I sniff those out.

Built your site with AI? Let's check it.

A lot of new sites are written mostly by AI tools, and they ship with the same holes: keys left in the code, pages anyone can open, forms that trust anything. Dibu tests sites like that, shows you what got through, and helps you fix it.

Offered now, while the Nod server is being built. More on the Server page.
